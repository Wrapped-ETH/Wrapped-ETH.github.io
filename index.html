What is Wrapped ETH?

WETH is ETH held in a smart contract and represented as an ERC-20 token for Ethereum apps. ethereum.org explains that deposits mint WETH and redemptions burn WETH for ETH.

Wrapped ETH token and contract

Wrapped ETH, usually shown as WETH, is the ERC-20 version of native ETH. The widely referenced Ethereum mainnet WETH contract is 0xC02aaA39b223FE8D0A0e5C4F27eAD9083C756Cc2, and it is used across DEXs, NFT markets, lending protocols, and vaults. WETH is not a separate asset with a separate monetary policy; it is created when ETH is deposited into the contract and redeemed when WETH is withdrawn. Users should verify the contract on Etherscan or CoinGecko before importing it, because fake WETH tokens are common phishing tools on new chains and low-liquidity pools.

Wrapped ETH price and peg

Wrapped ETH is intended to trade at 1 ETH because the canonical contract allows direct deposit and withdrawal at that ratio. In normal conditions, any market price difference is arbitraged by wrapping or unwrapping. Small price differences can appear in DEX interfaces due to routing, liquidity, gas, or stale quotes, but the base contract conversion is not a floating exchange rate. The main peg risks are user-side mistakes, fake tokens, chain-specific bridged assets, or interacting with a non-canonical wrapper. For Ethereum mainnet WETH, the core relationship is simple: one WETH can be redeemed for one ETH through the contract.

Wrapped ETH on Base, Arbitrum, and Optimism

WETH exists on many EVM chains, but each chain has its own contract, liquidity, and bridge path. On L2 networks such as Base, Arbitrum, and Optimism, WETH may represent ETH wrapped on that same chain or ETH that arrived through the network's bridge system. It cannot be assumed to be the same contract as Ethereum mainnet WETH. Before depositing to an exchange or protocol, users should confirm the supported network, token address, and bridge route. A correct ticker is not enough; network mismatch is one of the most common causes of failed or lost transfers.

Wrapped ETH FAQ

What is Wrapped ETH used for?

Wrapped ETH is used when a protocol needs ETH in ERC-20 token form. Common examples include DEX trading pairs, NFT bids, lending market collateral, liquidity pools, and automated contract strategies. Native ETH is still used for gas, but WETH gives smart contracts a standard token interface for balances, approvals, and transfers.

Is Wrapped ETH safer than ETH?

Wrapped ETH is not inherently safer than ETH. It adds smart-contract interaction and approval management while solving compatibility problems for DeFi. The canonical WETH contract is widely used, but users can still be harmed by fake tokens, phishing approvals, wrong-chain transfers, or malicious interfaces. ETH remains the native asset for gas and simple self-custody.

Does Wrapped ETH earn staking rewards?

No. WETH does not earn staking rewards simply because it is wrapped. It represents unstaked ETH in ERC-20 form. To earn staking rewards, users need a staking method or a liquid staking token such as stETH, rETH, or similar products. Those assets carry different reward mechanics and risks from WETH.

Can Wrapped ETH be bridged?

Yes, but bridging WETH introduces chain and bridge-specific assumptions. A bridge may lock or burn a token on one chain and mint or release a representation on another. Users should verify the bridge, destination token address, liquidity, and whether the destination asset is canonical WETH for that ecosystem or a bridged representation.

Why do token lists show different WETH addresses?

Token lists show different WETH addresses because every chain has its own contracts. Ethereum mainnet WETH has one address, while Arbitrum, Base, Optimism, Polygon, and other chains use different addresses. A token address should always be checked against the selected network and a trusted explorer before importing or transferring.

Can I pay gas with WETH?

On Ethereum and most EVM chains, gas is paid with the native gas asset, not with an ERC-20 token balance. WETH is useful inside protocols, but a wallet usually still needs native ETH or the chain's native gas token to submit transactions. Users may need to unwrap some WETH if they run out of gas funds.
