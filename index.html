Wrapped ETH (WETH)

Wrapped ETH (WETH) is a 1:1 ERC-20 representation of ETH that makes Ether compatible with DeFi apps built around the ERC-20 standard. In practice, WETH is the default base asset for DEX pools, routing, lending collateral, and many cross-protocol strategies.

Core idea: wrapping does not change your exposure — it changes the token standard. ETH ↔ WETH is typically a 1:1 conversion (plus gas), but you still need to verify the correct contract per chain.

What Is Wrapped ETH (WETH)?

ETH is the native asset of Ethereum, but it is not an ERC-20 token. Many DeFi protocols were designed around ERC-20 transfers, allowances (approvals), and standardized interfaces — so they use WETH as the ERC-20 version of ETH.

Why WETH Exists

  • ERC-20 compatibility: enables standard transfers and approvals.
  • DEX liquidity: most pools route through WETH pairs.
  • Protocol integrations: lending, borrowing, vaults, and aggregators often assume ERC-20.

What WETH Is Not

  • Not a yield token: WETH does not inherently earn yield.
  • Not a different exposure: it’s still ETH exposure (1:1).
  • Not universal across chains: “WETH” contracts vary by chain.

How Wrapping and Unwrapping Works

1
Wrap (ETH → WETH)

You deposit ETH into a wrapper contract and receive WETH 1:1 (gas applies).

2
Use (DeFi routing)

Trade through WETH pools, use as collateral, or interact with ERC-20-only apps.

3
Unwrap (WETH → ETH)

You redeem WETH back to ETH 1:1 (gas applies). Useful when you need native ETH.

Costs and Risks

Costs

  • Gas: wrap/unwrap and approvals cost gas.
  • Approvals: many DeFi actions require allowances.
  • Swap slippage: if you’re swapping (not wrapping), depth matters.

Risks

  • Wrong contract: biggest mistake across chains.
  • Approval risk: unlimited allowances increase blast radius.
  • Phishing: fake interfaces and lookalike domains.
Rule for real money: verify contract → test small → then scale.

Troubleshooting WETH

  • WETH not showing: wrong network or token not imported by contract address.
  • Can’t unwrap: you may be interacting with a different wrapped ETH contract on that chain.
  • Swap reverted: slippage too tight or route liquidity changed.
  • Balance “missing”: check explorer, token import, and wallet address.
Pro habit: keep tx hashes + contract addresses for every wrap/unwrap and swap.

Wrapped ETH (WETH) FAQ

Is WETH the same value as ETH? +
WETH is typically designed to be redeemable 1:1 for ETH. In normal conditions it tracks very closely, and wrapping/unwrapping is the direct 1:1 mechanism (gas applies).
Do I need to “buy” WETH? +
Often you don’t need to buy it — you can wrap ETH into WETH 1:1. Buying WETH via a swap can introduce slippage, so wrapping is usually cleaner if you already have ETH.
Why do DEXs use WETH instead of ETH? +
Many DeFi protocols were built around ERC-20 behavior (transfers + allowances). WETH is the ERC-20 representation of ETH, so it plugs into those contracts without special handling.
What’s the biggest WETH risk? +
Using the wrong contract on the wrong chain (or a fake token) is the most common high-impact mistake. Always verify contract address on a block explorer for your chain.
Is wrapping taxable? +
Tax treatment depends on jurisdiction. Some treat wrapping/unwrapping as non-taxable, others may treat it as a disposal. If taxes matter for your use case, check local guidance or a professional.
Why is my transaction pending? +
Usually network congestion or gas too low. Check the transaction in a block explorer and speed up/replace if supported.

Conclusion

Wrapped ETH (WETH) is a simple but critical building block in DeFi: it makes ETH behave like an ERC-20 token. If you verify the correct contract for your chain, keep approvals clean, and test small before scaling, you’ll avoid the most common mistakes.
